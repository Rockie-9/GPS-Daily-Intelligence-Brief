當最弱環節在牆外，GPS 守的是「看不見的依賴」
When the weakest link is outside the wall, GPS guards the "dependencies you cannot see"

台灣市占第一的企業簡訊與 OTP 平台 EVERY8D（母公司互動資通 Teamplus）自 5/21 起傳出遭勒索軟體入侵，核心服務癱瘓逾一週，銀行、電商、金流的身分驗證簡訊全面中斷。駭客在地下論壇兜售網路拓樸與資料庫樣本，其中竟包含五院、總統府、各部會與軍隊的真實簡訊日誌。F-ISAC 於 5/26 發布黃燈級（TLP:Amber）警訊，要求會員機構「先釐清暴露面，再應變處置」。台北市府證實 EVERY8D 為其短碼簡訊與內部通訊系統供應商，已啟動曝險清查。
Taiwan's top enterprise-SMS and OTP platform EVERY8D (parent: Teamplus) was reportedly hit by ransomware from 5/21, with core services down over a week — cutting off identity-verification SMS for banks, e-commerce, and payments. Hackers sold network topology and database samples on underground forums, including real message logs of the five branches of government, the Presidential Office, ministries, and the military. F-ISAC issued an Amber (TLP:Amber) alert on 5/26, requiring members to "clarify exposure first, then respond." Taipei City confirmed EVERY8D as its short-code SMS and internal-comms supplier, launching an exposure review.
為什麼這是今日主信號：EVERY8D 事件最深刻的啟示，是「最弱環節」原理——企業砸重金築起資安高牆，卻可能因一個第三方供應商的疏漏而全盤崩潰。簡訊平台是數位經濟少有人注意、卻牽一髮動全身的神經網路。對 GPS 而言，這個邏輯完全適用於實體世界：外包警衛、承包商、清潔與維運廠商——這些「牆外」的環節，往往是實體安全最被忽略的破口。
Why this is today's lead: The deepest lesson of the EVERY8D incident is the "weakest link" principle — a firm can spend heavily on a high security wall, yet collapse entirely through one third-party supplier's lapse. The SMS platform is a rarely-noticed nerve network where one strand moves the whole body. For GPS, this logic fully applies to the physical world: outsourced guards, contractors, cleaning and maintenance vendors — these "outside-the-wall" links are often physical security's most overlooked gaps.
GPS 透鏡
GPS LENS
我們習慣把安全想成「把自己的牆築高」，但 EVERY8D 提醒我們：你的安全強度，等於你最弱供應商的安全強度。對 GPS 而言，這意味實體安全的盤點不能只看自家門禁與監控，更要問：那些有權進出廠區的外部人員——承包商、維運、訪客——他們的背景審查、權限管理、行為紀錄，是否與內部員工同等嚴謹？最堅固的城牆，也擋不住一把被授權交出去的鑰匙。
We tend to think of security as "building our own wall higher," but EVERY8D reminds us: your security strength equals your weakest supplier's security strength. For GPS, this means a physical-security inventory can't only look at our own access and surveillance — it must ask: those external personnel authorized to enter our sites — contractors, maintenance, visitors — are their background checks, privilege management, and activity logs as rigorous as for internal staff? The strongest wall can't stop a key you authorized someone to hand over.

EVERY8D 遭駭：你的安全，等於你最弱供應商的安全
EVERY8D breached: your security equals your weakest supplier's security

事實：台灣市占第一的企業簡訊與 OTP 平台 EVERY8D（母公司互動資通 Teamplus）自 5/21 起遭勒索軟體入侵，核心服務癱瘓逾一週，銀行、證券、電商、金流的身分驗證簡訊全面中斷，每日逾一億封簡訊受波及。駭客在地下論壇以「SELLING」為題兜售上市公司網域控制器帳號與平台資料庫樣本，其中竟含五院、總統府、各部會與軍隊的真實簡訊日誌。金融資安資訊分享與分析中心（F-ISAC）於 5/26 發布黃燈級（TLP:Amber）資安事件警訊，要求會員機構「先釐清暴露面，再應變處置」，並提出五點防禦指引。台北市府證實 EVERY8D 為其短碼簡訊與內部通訊系統 TaipeiON 的供應商，已啟動曝險清查。

為什麼重要：這起事件最深刻的啟示是「最弱環節」原理——企業砸重金築起資安高牆，卻可能因一個第三方供應商的疏漏而全盤崩潰。更值得警惕的是：早在 2025 年 5 月，中芯數據就曾揭露攻擊者利用 Teamplus 通訊軟體入侵企業內部的供應鏈攻擊案例。一年後同一廠商再次成為破口，說明供應鏈風險不是「一次性事件」，而是需要持續監控的結構性問題。

What happened: Taiwan's top enterprise-SMS and OTP platform EVERY8D (parent: Teamplus) was hit by ransomware from 5/21, with core services down over a week — cutting off identity-verification SMS for banks, securities, e-commerce, and payments, affecting over 100 million daily messages. Hackers sold domain-controller accounts and database samples under "SELLING" on underground forums, including real message logs of the five branches of government, the Presidential Office, ministries, and the military. F-ISAC issued an Amber (TLP:Amber) alert on 5/26, requiring members to "clarify exposure first, then respond," with five defensive guidelines. Taipei City confirmed EVERY8D as supplier of its short-code SMS and internal-comms system TaipeiON, launching an exposure review.

Why it matters: The deepest lesson is the "weakest link" principle — a firm can spend heavily on a high security wall, yet collapse through one third-party supplier's lapse. More alarming: back in May 2025, CyCraft disclosed a supply-chain attack where attackers used Teamplus comms software to breach enterprises. The same vendor becoming a gap again a year later shows supply-chain risk isn't a "one-off event" but a structural problem requiring continuous monitoring.

GPS 解讀：我們習慣把安全想成「把自己的牆築高」，但 EVERY8D 提醒我們：你的安全強度，等於你最弱供應商的安全強度。對 GPS 而言，這完全適用於實體世界——那些有權進出廠區的外部人員（外包警衛、承包商、維運、清潔、訪客），他們的背景審查、權限管理、行為紀錄，是否與內部員工同等嚴謹？最堅固的城牆，也擋不住一把被授權交出去的鑰匙。實體安全的「供應鏈」，就是這些被授權進入我們空間的外部關係。
GPS Lens: We tend to think of security as "building our own wall higher," but EVERY8D reminds us: your security strength equals your weakest supplier's. For GPS, this fully applies to the physical world — those external personnel authorized to enter sites (outsourced guards, contractors, maintenance, cleaning, visitors): are their background checks, privilege management, and activity logs as rigorous as for internal staff? The strongest wall can't stop a key you authorized someone to hand over. Physical security's "supply chain" is exactly these authorized external relationships.
建議行動：換一個視角盤點「實體供應鏈」：列出所有有權進入廠區的外部單位，問三個問題——(1) 他們的人員異動，我們多快會知道？(2) 他們的門禁權限，是否在合約結束時確實撤銷？(3) 他們的行為，是否與內部員工一樣被記錄與稽核？這份清單不需技術背景就能起草，它本質是「信任邊界」的盤點。
Recommended action: Reframe by inventorying the "physical supply chain": list all external entities authorized to enter sites, and ask three questions — (1) How fast do we learn of their personnel changes? (2) Are their access privileges actually revoked at contract end? (3) Are their actions logged and audited like internal staff? This list needs no technical background — it's fundamentally an inventory of "trust boundaries."
可轉述句：「最堅固的城牆，也擋不住一把被授權交出去的鑰匙——實體安全的最弱環節，往往不是員工，是我們授權進入的外部夥伴。」
Cascade sentence: "The strongest wall can't stop a key you authorized someone to hand over — physical security's weakest link is often not employees, but the external partners we authorize in."

08a · 今日一個策略思考
08a · Today's Strategic Reflection
你的安全，等於你最弱供應商的安全
Your security equals your weakest supplier's security

EVERY8D 事件給所有人一個提醒：最堅固的城牆，也擋不住一把被授權交出去的鑰匙。我們習慣把安全想成「把自己顧好」，但真正的破口往往在「牆外」——那些我們依賴卻沒有直接掌控的外部關係。換個視角看你的工作範圍：有哪些事情，你假設「對方會處理好」卻從沒驗證過？最危險的信任，是「想當然爾」的信任。

EVERY8D offers everyone a reminder: the strongest wall can't stop a key you authorized someone to hand over. We tend to think of security as "taking care of our own," but the real gap is often "outside the wall" — those external relationships we rely on yet don't directly control. Reframe your scope: what do you assume "they'll handle" but have never verified? The most dangerous trust is the trust you take for granted.
